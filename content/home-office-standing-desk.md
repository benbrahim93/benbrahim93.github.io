Standing desks get sold on the standing. That's the easy part. The hard part, and the thing that decides whether you still use it after a month, is whether it stays still while you're up there working.

A wobbling desk is a desk you stop raising. So the buying decision is really about stability first and features second.

## How much should you actually spend?

Standing desk pricing falls into three bands, and the jump between them buys frame quality and motor life, not gadgets:

- **Under £150** — single motor, thin steel, narrow top. Fine for a laptop and one monitor if you keep it fairly low.
- **£200–£400** — the sensible middle. Dual motors, a sturdier frame, memory presets and a worktop you don't have to think about. Most home offices should land here.
- **£400–£800+** — heavier frames, wider tops, quieter motors, longer warranties. You're paying for stability at full height and a motor that lasts.

What you're *not* getting by spending more: a healthier back on its own. A standing desk only helps if you change position regularly. It's a tool for that, not the cure.

## The features that matter

**Frame construction, because this is what causes wobble.** Height comes from telescoping legs, and where two tubes overlap the joint flexes. A three-stage leg goes higher but adds overlap points to flex, so a good three-stage frame uses stiffer steel than a good two-stage one. Thicker tube resists sway far more than thin tube.

**Leg count and crossbars.** Two-leg T-frames are the norm and are fine. Four-leg frames are steadier at the top of their range but eat floor space. A rear crossbar ties the legs together and takes out most of the front-to-back rock.

**Mass is your friend.** A heavy worktop low down acts as ballast and damps sway. A desk is a lever: the higher you raise the top, the more a sideways push at the keyboard is amplified. A solid 25mm top on a good frame beats a flimsy 18mm top on the same frame.

**Single motor versus dual motor.** One motor driving both legs is fine while the desk is narrow. The wider it gets, the more it twists and the more strain on that motor. Dual motors put one on each leg, so a 140cm or 160cm top rises level and lasts longer.

**Memory presets and anti-collision.** Presets store your sitting and standing heights at one touch — the feature you'll use daily. Anti-collision stops the desk when it meets an obstacle, saving your knees and your chair. Treat their absence as a warning about the build.

**Weight capacity, read carefully.** The advertised figure is the lift the motor can manage, not what to pile on. Real loads add up — monitors on arms, a PC tower, speakers. Use well under half the stated capacity and the frame feels far more solid. Noise counts too: a quiet desk is roughly fridge-hum level.

**Worktop size against your room.** Measure wall to window, and how far the desk can stand off the wall before a radiator or socket gets in the way. A 160cm top is generous until it blocks the only window. Measure the floor you'll walk through, not just the wall.

**Cable management.** Wires hanging off a rising desk snag and pull. A tray underneath stops cables fighting the frame.

**The sit-stand ratio nobody keeps.** Most people plan to stand half the day and end up standing a fraction of it. Build the habit slowly — an hour, then more — and accept the desk will mostly sit. Buy for the wobble-free sit; standing is a bonus.

## What to avoid

- **Big and cheap.** A wide desk on a thin single-motor frame wobbles at height and racks as it moves. A smaller top on a good frame is the better buy every time.
- **Thin steel dressed up.** A chunky-looking column can hide a narrow inner tube. Check the stated leg stages and the weight, not the photo.
- **Buying the top as an afterthought.** A hollow or too-thin worktop adds nothing to stability. It's ballast as much as a surface.
- **Ignoring the motor warranty.** The frame rarely fails; the motor and control box do. A clear, long warranty on the electrics is worth more than a fancier control panel.

## Bottom line

Pick the desk width your room can actually take, then buy the best frame that fits it — thick steel, dual motors if it's wide, a crossbar, and a solid top that adds weight. Check the motor warranty before you check the colour. A solid smaller desk beats a shaky big one, and it's the one you'll still be raising next year.
