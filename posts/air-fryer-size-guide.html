Air Fryers: What Size You Actually Need (and What's Overkill)

Air fryers get sold on one promise: crispy food, less oil, faster than the oven. That part is true. The problem isn't the technology, it's the shopping — most people either buy a model that's too small to cook a real meal in, or a huge one that eats half their worktop and sits unused by February.

So let's fix the sizing question first, because it's the decision you can't undo.

How much should you actually spend?

Air fryer pricing falls into three clear bands, and the jump between them buys capacity and build quality, not better cooking:

  • Under £60** — single-person cooking, or a second appliance for sides. Expect one basket, manual dials, and a heating element that lasts a few years of regular use.
  • £60–£120** — the sensible middle. Dual baskets, digital controls, more even heat. If you cook for two or more, this is where the value is.
  • £120–£250** — larger families, or people who want to replace the oven. You're paying for capacity and consistency, not magic.

What you're *not* getting by spending more: meaningfully healthier food. All air fryers cook with hot circulating air. A £250 unit and a £70 unit both make chips with a spoonful of oil.

The features that matter

Capacity in litres — but read it properly. Manufacturers quote the total basket volume, and you can only fill it about two-thirds full or the air can't circulate. A "5 litre" basket realistically cooks for two. A rough rule that works: 1 litre per person, then add one more. Two adults and a kid means a 4-litre minimum, and dual-basket 8-litre units are the ones that can cook a whole meal at once.

Physical size, which nobody mentions. Measure your worktop *and* your cupboard clearance before buying. Air fryers need open space above them to vent. A machine that technically fits but sits under a low shelf will underperform and steam your food instead of crisping it.

Dual baskets. One of the genuinely useful upgrades. Protein in one side, vegetables in the other, finish at the same time. If you're feeding more than one person, this matters more than brand.

Wattage. Around 1400–1800W is normal for a home unit. Higher wattage mainly means it reaches temperature faster. If your kitchen is on the same circuit as a kettle, a toaster and a microwave, that's worth thinking about — but it's rarely the deciding factor.

Ease of cleaning. The basket coating and whether the parts are dishwasher-safe will decide how often you actually use it. This is the single most underrated feature, and the reason many air fryers end up in a cupboard.

What to avoid

  • Buying for the family you might have.** Buy for the plates you cook today. An oversized unit cooks small portions unevenly.
  • Non-stick coatings you scrub hard.** It shortens the life of the basket dramatically. Soak, then wash soft.
  • Windowless, lightless baskets at the small end.** You'll be opening it constantly to check, which dumps the heat.
  • "Air fryer ovens" if you want speed.** The flip-door oven styles are versatile but they preheat and cook more like a small oven, which is the thing you were trying to escape.
  • Trusting "serves 4" marketing.** It usually means four chicken wings.
