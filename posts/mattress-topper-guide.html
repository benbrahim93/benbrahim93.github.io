Mattress Toppers: Why Yours Flattens and How to Pick One That Lasts

By Brahim Ben Sliman · Updated 2026-10-14 · 6 min read

A mattress topper is a soft layer that sits on the mattress to change how the bed feels. For the right problem it's one of the better-value buys in the bedroom: it softens a too-firm bed, adds warmth under the duvet, and gives a fresh surface over an older mattress. For the wrong problem it's money laid over furniture that needs replacing: the one thing a topper cannot do is rescue a sagging mattress. A topper lies over the dip and wears out along the line of the dip. The honest limit of any topper is the depth of the sag underneath it.

The second honesty item: every type flattens eventually. If your last topper went flat within a year, the type you bought is the reason, and that's fixable at the point of purchase. Here's how to choose better.

How much should you actually spend?

Toppers fall into three bands, and the sensible value sits in the middle:

  • Roughly £25–£50** — thin foam and basic fibre toppers. Fine for a guest bed; rarely enough to change a bed you sleep in nightly.
  • Roughly £50–£130** — the sensible middle. Proper-depth memory foam and the better fibre toppers. Most homes should shop here.
  • Roughly £130–£250 and up** — latex and premium builds. Worth it if you sleep hot and want the longest life.

If you're buying a second or third topper to patch the same bed, do the arithmetic: the combined cost is creeping towards a mattress price — and the mattress was the real answer.

The features that matter

The type, in plain terms. Each type trades feel against warmth and lifespan:

  • Memory foam** — soft, slow to respond, moulds to you. Good for easing pressure at hips and shoulders, but it sleeps warm.
  • Gel-infused memory foam** — foam with a cooling gel added; the claims are of mixed value in practice. It's still foam underneath.
  • Latex** — bouncier, cooler, pricier and heavier. Usually the longest-lived of the types.
  • Down, feather and duck-down alternatives** — fluffy and warm, but they flatten and need regular shaking to lift again.
  • Hybrid or fibre** — the middle ground: more forgiving than foam, cooler, and often washable, but it flattens before foam does.

Thickness, the number that decides everything. A 5cm topper rarely transforms a bed — it compresses under you and you're back to feeling the mattress within weeks. Around 7–8cm is where a topper genuinely changes the feel of the bed. Deeper is mostly extra bulk.

UK bed sizes. Double is 4ft6 (135cm), king is 5ft (150cm), super-king is 6ft (180cm). Buy the size that matches the mattress, not the room or the frame — frames overhang by inches, and depth varies far more than width.

The skirt depth. A topper skirt has to reach round and under the mattress. A thick or orthopaedic mattress needs a deep skirt; a shallow one pings off the corners within a night.

Temperature. Memory foam sleeps warm: it hugs the body and traps heat. Hot sleepers should head for latex or fibre. If you sleep with a leg out of the duvet, foam is the wrong material at any price.

Keeping it in place. Straps or corner anchors hold a topper still; cheap ones slide. One that bunches up by 3am is worse than no topper.

Washing. A removable, washable cover is worth paying for — the surface you sleep on collects everything. An unwashable fibre topper is a hygiene problem with a lifespan, not an investment.

Protector or topper? A mattress protector is a thin waterproof sheet guarding the mattress from spills. A topper is the comfort layer. Different jobs — the protector goes under; it doesn't replace.

What to avoid

  • A topper over a sagging mattress.** It follows the dip and flattens along it; only the mattress fixes the sag.
  • 5cm toppers when you want real change.** They're a tweak, not a transformation.
  • Toppers with no straps or skirt.** On a smooth mattress they walk to the floor within a week.
  • Foam if you sleep hot**, whatever the gel marketing says.
  • Fibre toppers that can't be washed**, especially for children's beds or anyone with allergies.

Bottom line

A topper is the right buy when the mattress is sound but the feel is wrong: too firm, too cold, or past its freshest. Pick roughly 7–8cm of the material that suits how you sleep — memory foam for pressure relief, latex or fibre if you run hot, the fluffy types only if you enjoy plumping them. Fit it with straps, and take the lifespan honestly: foam typically holds its shape for a few years, a fluffy one wants replacing far sooner. But if the mattress is dipping and you sleep in the crater, stop spending on layers. A topper hides the problem beautifully for a few months, then charges you for it again. The new mattress is the uncomfortable, dear-looking, genuinely cheaper answer in the long run.
