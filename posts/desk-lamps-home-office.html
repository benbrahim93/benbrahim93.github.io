Home Office Lighting: Why Your Screen Is Fine and Your Room Isn't

Your screen is probably the brightest thing in the room, and that's the problem. By the end of a working day your eyes ache and your head feels tight, and most people blame the monitor. Usually the monitor is fine — it's the room around it that's too dark.

Bright screen, dark room, and your eyes constantly readjust between the two. That contrast is what tires them out. Fix the room and much of the end-of-day strain goes with it.

How much should you actually spend?

Desk lamp pricing falls into three bands, and the jump between them buys light quality and dimming control, not brightness:

  • Under £20** — fixed brightness, often a single cool-white setting, and a driver that may flicker. Fine as an extra, not as your main light.
  • £25–£60** — the sensible middle. Dimmable, adjustable colour temperature, a high colour-rendering index and flicker-free electronics. Most home offices should land here.
  • £60–£150+** — better diffusers, wider dimming down to a genuinely low setting, sturdier clamps.

What you're *not* getting by spending more: a brighter lamp. Past a point, brightness isn't the problem — control is.

The features that matter

Ambient and task light, because you need both. Task light is the pool of light on your desk, over your keyboard and papers. Ambient light is the general light that stops the walls and screen edge being the only bright things. A desk lamp alone in a dark room is still a contrast problem — you want the room lit softly *and* the desk lit well.

Colour temperature, in kelvin. Kelvin is just how warm or cool the light looks. Around 2700K is warm, like an old bulb, good for evening. Around 4000K is neutral, close to daylight, good for focused work. Above 5000K is cool and blue-ish. Cool in the morning matches your body clock; warm is gentler before bed. If the lamp is fixed, 4000K is the safe middle.

Lumens, not watts. Watts told you brightness back when bulbs were all the same technology. LEDs broke that: a 9W LED can match an old 60W bulb. Lumens is the real light output, and roughly 400 to 800 on the desk is plenty for reading and typing.

CRI, and why text can feel fuzzy on cheap LEDs. Colour rendering index runs to 100, which is sunlight. Cheap LEDs often sit in the 70s, which makes black text on white paper look washed out, so your eyes work harder. Aim for CRI 90 or higher.

Glare, and where your monitor sits. Glare is light bouncing off a shiny surface into your eyes. A glossy desk reflects the lamp and the window back at you; a matte desk is kinder. Keep the monitor out of the window's reflection and angle the lamp head down and away.

Why a ceiling light alone is the worst setup. A single ceiling light puts a bright patch overhead and shadows everywhere else, including across your hands. Fine as background fill, but alone it makes an office feel like a waiting room.

Flicker is the hidden irritant. Some cheap LEDs dim by switching on and off very fast, which can give a faint flicker you don't consciously see but your eyes register — a real cause of strain and headaches. Wave a pen under a lamp: a trail of strobing means walk away.

Cable routing, and clamp versus base. Base lamps take up the most valuable desk space and are easy to knock. Clamp lamps bolt to the rear edge and free the worktop.

Dimming matters more than peak brightness. You'll rarely want full output. What you want is to bring the light down smoothly at the end of the day and set a level that matches the screen. A lamp that dims to a genuinely low setting, flicker-free, is worth more than one that goes slightly brighter.

What to avoid

  • Bright screen, dark room.** Light the room, not just the desk, so your eyes aren't fighting the contrast all day.
  • Fixed cool-white lamps.** 6000K with no adjustment is harsh for anything but a morning boost.
  • Low-CRI cheap LEDs.** They make fine detail and text harder to read than they need to be.
  • Non-dimmable lamps as your main light.** One flat brightness is wrong half the day.

Bottom line

Your eyes don't want a brighter screen in a darker room. Light the room softly, put a dimmable, flicker-free, high-CRI lamp over the desk, and set the colour temperature cool in the morning and warm in the evening. Spend in the middle band — that's where light quality and control sit, and it's the upgrade you'll feel by the end of the day.
